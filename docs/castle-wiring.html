Three light zones,
two speakers, one Feather

Drawn to the board’s real header layout — 16 positions on the left, 12 on the right, both ending flush at the far end, so you can count down to the pin you want. Each light zone gets its own data pin, because an RGBW fixture and an RGB one physically cannot share a wire. Both amplifiers ride the same three I²S lines and cost no extra GPIO — though two of those three are on the opposite header from the third.

No resistors in the parts box yet? For first light on the bench, both kinds are skippable: the 470 Ωs damp cable reflections, and with every data lead under ~30 cm there are none worth damping — wire 1Y/2Y/3Y straight to DIN and add the resistors (fixture end) when the leads grow into the castle. Leaving the amps' SD unconnected selects (L+R)/2, this show's mono at worst −6 dB — the 100 kΩ to 5 V restores full level later. Four ways this goes wrong

No data

Frozen bright garbage means no data ever arrived

A pixel that gets 5 V but never a valid frame powers up showing random contents — a blinding, frozen mix of colours and white, different on every pixel. One fixture all lit, another with a single lit LED: that is noise, not a fault map. If the picture has not changed since power-on, stop debugging fixtures and firmware — walk the data path instead: OE legs to ground, 5 V on pin 14, A0/A2/A4 into 1A/2A/3A, and one shared ground.

Ground

One ground, or nothing works

ESP32, level shifter, both amps and the LED supply must all share a ground. A data line is a voltage relative to ground; two subsystems with unjoined grounds have no shared idea of what “high” means. This is the usual cause of random flicker, and it looks exactly like a software bug.

Colour

RGBW and RGB cannot share a chain

An RGBW pixel takes 32 bits, an RGB pixel 24, and a chain is one shift register with no framing — so one RGB fixture halfway down an RGBW chain shifts every downstream bit by eight. Your FeatherWing 4×8 and mini PCBs are RGB-only. That is why each zone gets its own pin.

Supply

An underpowered rig crashes, it doesn't dim

A strike pulls the worst case for about 150 ms. On a small supply that sag resets the ESP32 mid-scene — so the castle fails only during the good bits. Do not run pixels off the Feather's USB pin; that trace is sized for the board. (One exception: the 26-pixel first-light rig on a 2 A+ wall brick — docs/WIRING.md §0. The full castle, never.)

Build order

Each step proves the one before it.

  1. Grounds first. Supply, Feather, shifter, both amps. Check continuity before anything is powered.
  2. Power the shifter alone. 5 V on pin 14, GND on pin 7, and 1OE/2OE/3OE to ground. Confirm 5 V at pin 14.
  3. One zone. Tower L only: GPIO18 → 1A, 1Y → 470 Ω → DIN, plus 5 V, GND and the capacitor. You already know what a working Jewel looks like, so this is the honest test of the shifter.
  4. The other two. Same pattern on GPIO16 and GPIO14. Set the rig in the cue desk first so the firmware knows the counts.
  5. One amp. SD to 5 V through 100 kΩ. Play a scene and confirm full volume, not a quiet one.
  6. The second amp. The same three signal wires, tapped off the first. Both should now be equally loud.
  7. Measure the real peak. Run Storm with a meter on the 5 V bus and compare it against the desk's estimate before you close the enclosure.
